    Notes & sources

    How this model reads the text

    Sources

    The model follows Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam Canto 5, chapters 16 to 26, in the Bhaktivedanta Book Trust translation, with a few verses from Cantos 2, 3 and 10. Where Canto 5 gives no figure, the Viṣṇu Purāṇa (Book 2, chapters 5 and 7) fills the gap. Every figure in the side panel carries a tag: SB for the Bhāgavatam, VP for the Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Calculated for a number worked out from the text's own figures, and Purport for Śrīla Prabhupāda's commentary.

    All distances are in yojanas. The BBT translation converts one yojana to about 8 miles; other traditions use other values.

    Two views, one cutaway

    Schematic compresses distances so every tier can be seen and clicked. The order of everything is exact. The spacing is not: each island and ocean ring grows by one step for every doubling of its real width, and heights are stretched near the disc and squeezed far above it.

    True proportions draws radii and heights on one linear scale, 1 unit to 1,000,000 yojanas. The seven islands shrink to the inner fifth of the disc, the golden land becomes the widest band, and every planet from the Sun to Dhruvaloka sits within 3.8 million yojanas of the disc.

    Cutaway removes the quarter of the large layers nearest you, so you can see the lower worlds and the water inside. The missing quarter turns with you as you move around the model. Nothing in the text is being removed; it only lets you see inside. It is off while you explore a single world.

    Moving around. Use the Rotate and Pan buttons at the bottom left. In Pan, dragging moves the view without turning or zooming; holding Space pans too. Drag the coloured height ruler, use Shift + scroll or PageUp/PageDown to glide up and down the universe. Scrolling zooms toward whatever is under the mouse. Arrow keys pan, F frames the selected item, Home resets the view.

    Spotlight. Clicking anything dims the rest of the model and draws your choice bright and whole on top (the cutaway never slices it), with a trail at the top showing where you are. A selected planet is followed as it moves. If you zoom or pan so far that your choice leaves the screen, a “Bring it back” button appears. Clicking empty space or dragging never clears your choice; press Esc or pick “Universe” in the trail.

    Layers shows or hides parts of the model itself. Panels shows or hides each panel of the page separately, so you can clear the view. Drone plays a soft tanpura-style sound in the background.

    Symbols. Trees, lotuses, cities, rivers and serpents inside each world are symbols placed where the text mentions them. They are not to scale, and where the text doesn't say where something is, the placement is only illustrative.

    Drawing choices that are not in the text

    1. Islands are drawn slightly raised above the oceans so land and water read apart. The text gives no heights for them.
    2. The four lakes around Meru are named in 5.16.13 but not placed; their positions are illustrative. The same holds for cities, trees and serpents inside each world, and the seven peaks on the middle islands.
    3. The nine varṣas are tinted different colours only so they can be told apart.
    4. The Gaṅgā's route between the stops named in 5.17.1–4, and where it enters the shell, are illustrative.
    5. In Day mode the Sun circles Meru once a day and the disc is shaded into a lit and a dark half, with light ending at Lokāloka (5.20.37, 5.21.8–9). Placing the Sun's northern course toward Meru and its southern course away from it is an interpretation.
    6. The band of twelve signs shown with a planet starts from an arbitrary point, so the sign it shows is for illustration.

    Where sources differ, or the translation needs care

    1. Venus and Mercury. SB 5.22.12–13 puts Venus 200,000 yojanas above the nakṣatras and Mercury 200,000 above Venus. The Viṣṇu Purāṇa (2.7) has the reverse. The model follows the Bhāgavatam.
    2. Spacing of the planets. By SB 5.22–23, Dhruvaloka is about 3,700,000 yojanas above the Sun. The Viṣṇu Purāṇa gives 1,400,000. The model follows the Bhāgavatam.
    3. The Sun's height. Canto 5 places the Sun at the vertical middle of the universe (5.20.43) and says it travels on top of Mānasottara (5.20.30), but gives no height above Bhū-maṇḍala. The model uses the Viṣṇu Purāṇa's 100,000 yojanas. The mileage "above earth" added in the BBT translation of 5.22.13–17 implies 200,000 instead.
    4. Jambūdvīpa's width. The BBT translation of 5.16.5 reads "one million yojanas"; 5.16.7 and 5.20.2 give 100,000, which every other figure depends on.
    5. Brahmapurī. The BBT translation of 5.16.28 gives ten million yojanas a side, which cannot fit on Meru's 32,000-yojana summit. The chapter summary gives 10,000.
    6. The Sun's orbit. Mānasottara lies 15,750,000 yojanas from Meru's centre by the widths in SB 5.20, a circle of about 98,960,000 yojanas. SB 5.21.19 gives the orbit as 95,100,000.
    7. The seven upper worlds. SB 2.5.38–39 names all seven, Bhūr, Bhuvar, Svar, Mahar, Jana, Tapa and Satya, placing them from the legs to the head of the universal form. SB 5.21.2–3 gives the order of the first three: the earthly sphere below, heaven above, and between them the sky called antarikṣa, with the Sun in its midst (also 5.20.43). SB 5.20.37 makes Lokāloka the outer border of the first three, so they share one width; the Viṣṇu Purāṇa agrees that Bhuvar is as broad as Bhūr. Canto 5 gives no heights for where one world ends and the next begins. The boundaries drawn here, Bhuvar up to the Sun and Svar from the Sun to Dhruvaloka, and the heights of Mahar-, Jana-, Tapo- and Satyaloka, come from the Viṣṇu Purāṇa 2.7. SB 5.24.5–6 also uses the words in a narrower sense: the earth reaches as high as large birds fly, and antarikṣa as high as wind blows and clouds float. Which three worlds are destroyed at the end of Brahmā's day, and who moves where, comes from SB 3.11.29–30.
    8. Lower worlds and water. The depth of each lower world (10,000 yojanas) is from the Viṣṇu Purāṇa 2.5. Canto 5 gives no depth for the Garbhodaka water; Prabhupāda's purports describe the lower half of the universe as filled with it.
    9. The Sītā. The BBT translation of 5.17.6 says the Sītā reaches the salt ocean "in the west", though it flows east through Gandhamādana and Bhadrāśva. The model draws it reaching the eastern shore.
    10. Interpretations. Linking our planet with Bhārata-varṣa comes from Prabhupāda's purports. Reading Bhū-maṇḍala as the plane of the planets' orbits comes from Richard L. Thompson (Sadāpūta Dāsa). Both are marked as interpretations wherever they appear.
